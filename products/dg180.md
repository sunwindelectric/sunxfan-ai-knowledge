### FAQ

**Q: When is a 330–360 Pa, high-airflow blower the right choice?**

A: When the resistance is short and honest: condensing-unit discharge, appliance exhaust up a short stack, cabinet air exchange through a grille and maybe one bend. 780–960 m³/h at 330–360 Pa moves serious volume through such paths without paying the noise and power premium of a high-pressure wheel fighting an open system. The mistake to avoid is the reverse: a 700 Pa-class blower on a 150 Pa system rides far off its design point, wastes energy and sounds harsh.

**Q: 4-speed taps or 0-10V/PWM — how do I choose?**

A: The 4-speed taps serve uncontrolled installs: a technician sets one point with a jumper or switch — kitchen boost, condenser duty, appliance exhaust — and the fixed steps guarantee repeatable airflow and noise. 0-10V/PWM is for when a controller or BMS will trim speed against a sensor. On fixed duty, land the tap on your actual system curve rather than defaulting to the fastest: each step down buys several dB(A) and a cubic saving in power.

**Q: What does the PP impeller contribute here?**

A: Glass-fibre-filled polypropylene keeps wheel mass low: gentle bearing loads over years of continuous running and little start inertia for the EC motor. It is intrinsically corrosion-proof — salt air, humidity and cleaning-agent traces that pit coated steel leave it untouched, which is why appliance and marine-air-conditioning duty favours it. The honest trade is temperature: respect the −25 ~ +60 °C envelope; for airstreams that live near that ceiling, specify a steel-wheel blower instead.
