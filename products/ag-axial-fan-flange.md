### FAQ

**Q: What are the installation advantages of an EC 280×280×80 mm axial fan with flange?**

A: **EC axial fan, 280×280×80 mm, with flange, 230V, and 0-10V/PWM speed control**, belonging to the category of **high-airflow, slim-profile, intelligently controllable** industrial-grade cooling and ventilation fans.

The flange structure facilitates mounting through openings in enclosures or wall panels. The 80mm slim depth greatly saves internal equipment space, while the EC motor combined with 0-10V/PWM speed control enables on-demand air supply and low energy consumption.

### Installation Advantages Summary

-   **Flange:** Provides robust four-corner/perimeter bolt fixing with high fit accuracy, easy sealing, and leakage prevention.

-   **80mm Ultra-Slim Depth:** Suitable for "flat" equipment structures with stringent axial space requirements.

-   **0-10V / PWM:** The core of intelligent control, capable of directly interfacing with sensors, PLCs, or smart main control boards to achieve stepless speed regulation, soft start, and fault feedback.

**Q: Flange or guard-ring mounting — which AG variant do I need?**

A: The flange version bolts into a cut-out and carries its weight through the flange plate — the natural choice for wall and partition openings, condenser casings and duct transitions. The guard-ring build (AS series) is for open discharge: a ring with integrated finger protection, free-blowing over coils or in windows. Decide by what surrounds the fan: a structure that can take a flange gives the stiffest, quietest mount; free air wants a guard.

**Q: How much static pressure can an axial fan realistically deliver?**

A: This one is rated to 300 Pa — a typical ceiling for a well-made axial of this class before efficiency and noise degrade badly. That covers coils, louvres and short ducts, the native duty of axial fans. Beyond roughly 300-500 Pa the honest answer is a centrifugal: pushing an axial into high pressure puts it in stall-prone territory with a harsh tonal signature. Pick the fan type from the system resistance first, then the model.
