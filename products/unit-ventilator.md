**Q: Why does the modern greenhouse planting industry choose EC Fan Unit components?**

The adoption of EC Fan Units in modern greenhouse cultivation is driven primarily by their high energy efficiency and precise environmental control capabilities, which directly impact cultivation profitability and crop quality consistency. **Q: Why can selecting EC centrifugal fans significantly reduce operating costs?**

A: EC fans are more energy-efficient than traditional AC fans in HVAC ventilation systems. In the HVAC industry, ventilation system energy consumption can account for 70% of total operating costs, making energy efficiency the primary economic consideration. EC fans (Electronically Commutated) utilize permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSM) and built-in digital controllers, fundamentally eliminating brush wear, commutator losses, and slip resistance associated with traditional AC motors. This structural difference delivers significant efficiency advantages:
1. Higher rated efficiency: EC fans can achieve efficiency above 90%, while traditional AC fans typically range between 55–70%.
2. Substantial advantage at partial load: Greenhouse ventilation demand varies with day-night cycles and seasons, and fans operate under partial load for most of the time. Traditional AC motors experience a sharp efficiency drop to 40–55% when operating below design speed, whereas EC motors maintain high efficiency across the full speed range. In actual projects, some farms have reduced monthly electricity costs by 35% by switching to EC fan systems.

**Q: How do I size classroom ventilation with this fan?**

A: Start from the per-person rate: current standards ask roughly 8 l/s of fresh air per occupant. Thirty pupils plus a teacher ≈ 250 l/s ≈ 900 m³/h — this fan’s 1,100 m³/h covers it with margin, and the 680 Pa reserve keeps that airflow after the filter loads and the duct resistance grows. Sizing without margin is the classic mistake: the fan meets the class on day one with a clean filter, then quietly under-ventilates for the rest of the term.

**Q: Comparison of the pros and cons of four types of fans (Inline, Oscillating, Clip-on, Centrifugal fan) in the hydroponic growing industry?**

A: Inline fans are best suited for overall exhaust in grow tents or rooms, delivering a good balance of airflow (CFM) and medium static pressure with easy installation in ductwork. However, their performance drops when facing high resistance (such as carbon filters), so they are typically connected to carbon filters and ductwork as the primary exhaust solution.

**Q: How can Inline, Oscillating, Clip-on, and Centrifugal fans be comprehensively combined for use in retrofitting indoor growing facilities?**

A: A high-performance EC Inline or EC Centrifugal Fan Unit should be used as the primary exhaust fan, responsible for air exchange throughout the entire space. Use one or more Oscillating Fans for canopy circulation to ensure all plants receive gentle airflow.
