# Dual-Inlet vs. Single-Inlet Blowers: Pros & Cons

Choosing between single-inlet and dual-inlet centrifugal blowers is one of the most
common decisions engineers face when specifying HVAC and industrial ventilation
equipment. Both designs have clear strengths and trade-offs, and the right choice
depends on airflow demand, static pressure, available space and total cost of
ownership.

## Quick Answer

A **single-inlet centrifugal blower** draws air from one side — it is compact,
affordable and best for focused, localized airflow. A **dual-inlet blower**
draws from both sides simultaneously — roughly double the inlet area delivers
more airflow, higher static pressure and quieter operation for larger ducted
systems, at a higher upfront cost.

## How the Two Designs Differ

- **Single-inlet blowers** draw air from one side and deliver a focused, localized
  airflow stream.
- **Dual-inlet blowers** draw air from both sides simultaneously, producing a more
  balanced, evenly distributed airflow across larger duct networks.

| Feature | Single-Inlet Blower | Dual-Inlet Blower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air intake | One side | Both sides of the motor |
| Airflow pattern | Focused, localized stream | Balanced, evenly distributed |
| Static pressure capability | Moderate | Higher — stable through filters, bends, long ducts |
| Footprint | More compact | Larger |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Noise & vibration | Noticeable | Smoother, quieter |
| Best for | Embedded cooling, compact units | Ducted HVAC, industrial ventilation |

## Advantages of Single-Inlet Blowers

- **Compact installation** — a streamlined design that fits easily into restricted
  spaces.
- **Lower upfront cost** — fewer structural components translate to a more
  affordable purchase price.
- **Simple maintenance** — a straightforward structure makes servicing and
  replacement easier.
- **Efficient for targeted airflow** — well suited to applications with moderate
  airflow demand.

## Limitations of Single-Inlet Blowers

- **Lower airflow capacity** compared to dual-inlet designs.
- **Uneven air distribution** in extensive or complex ductwork systems.
- **Reduced static pressure** performance when facing heavy filtration or long
  duct runs.

## Limitations of Dual-Inlet Blowers

- **Larger footprint** — dual inlets and bigger overall dimensions require more
  installation space.
- **Higher initial cost** — the advanced structure and larger size carry a higher
  upfront price tag.
- **More complex maintenance** when both inlets need simultaneous servicing or
  access is restricted.

## Static Pressure Capability

Single-inlet blowers perform well under moderate static pressure but can lose
efficiency against extensive ductwork or heavily filtered systems. Dual-inlet
blowers are built to withstand higher resistance, maintaining stable performance
through filters, bends and long duct runs.

## Airflow Distribution

Because dual-inlet blowers draw air from both sides, they supply more uniform
airflow across broad duct networks — an advantage for large systems where consistent
ventilation is essential. Single-inlet designs are better suited to targeted,
localized airflow.

## Cost Considerations

- **Initial investment:** single-inlet models are more budget friendly upfront.
- **Operating costs:** dual-inlet blowers cost more at the outset but often deliver
  better long-term value through energy savings and stable performance.
- **Maintenance:** simpler structures generally mean fewer maintenance demands over
  the equipment lifecycle.

## IP55 Protection

Many manufacturers offer both single-inlet and dual-inlet centrifugal blowers with
IP55-rated protection against dust and moisture — a key consideration for harsh or
humid environments.

## How to Choose

- **Airflow demand** — larger systems favor dual-inlet; smaller systems suit
  single-inlet.
- **Static pressure & ductwork** — high resistance or complex ducts call for
  dual-inlet.
- **Noise & vibration** — dual-inlet blowers generally run smoother and quieter.
- **Budget vs. lifetime cost** — single-inlet saves upfront; dual-inlet saves over
  time.
- **Application environment** — match the blower to the operating conditions.

In short: choose single-inlet for compact, budget-sensitive, moderate-airflow
applications; choose dual-inlet for high-airflow, high-static-pressure or
distribution-critical HVAC and industrial systems.
